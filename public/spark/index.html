Candidate Solutions to Case Study Questions (Q1 to Q10)

Concise, human-style evaluation based on data analysis

Role: Data Analyst / MIS Officer
1. SALES & BUSINESS PERFORMANCE Questions 1 & 2

Q1: Is this a healthy improvement in business performance? What concerns or questions would you raise?

No, this is NOT a healthy improvement. While top-line sales grew from NPR 3.60 Cr to 8.90 Cr (+147%), two severe concerns exist:

  • Outstanding Receivables grew from 6.20 Cr to 10.80 Cr (+74%): At 10.8 Cr, uncollected money is larger than September's entire monthly sales (8.9 Cr). The company is selling on paper, but cash is trapped.
  • Discounts are eating profits (2.0% → 5.0%): Vehicle gross margins are thin (6.4% to 8.7%). Offering a 5% discount wipes out 57% to 78% of gross margin. We are buying volume by giving away company profit.

Q2: What additional KPIs or information would you want to see?

1. Cash Collections vs Credit Sales: Actual cash deposited in bank vs invoiced sales.
2. Days Sales Outstanding (DSO): Velocity of converting receivables to cash.
3. Net Realized Margin per Unit: Gross profit minus discount, bank LC interest, and vehicle holding costs.
4. Product Sales Mix: Breakdown of sales by model (Machinery vs Trucks vs EV Vans).
5. Customer Credit Limit Status: Are vehicles still delivered to customers with overdue balances?

2. MARKETING PERFORMANCE Questions 3 & 4

Q3: Do you agree that marketing performance has improved because leads increased from 180 to 680?

No, I do not agree. While top-of-funnel leads grew, lead quality and efficiency collapsed:

  • Qualified Lead % dropped: In April, 38.9% of leads were qualified (70/180). In September, only 25.0% qualified (170/680)—a 13.9% drop. 510 out of 680 leads were junk.
  • True Conversion halved: Lead-to-sale conversion dropped from 6.67% to 3.68%.
  • Cost per Qualified Lead (CPQL) rose: Increased from NPR 3,571 to NPR 4,118 (+15.3%). Marketing is paying more per real buyer.

Q4: What marketing KPIs to calculate and prioritize?

Calculate: Lead Qualification Rate %, Cost Per Qualified Lead (CPQL), Lead-to-Sale Conversion Rate, and CAC per unit.
Priority for Management: CPQL and Lead Qualification Rate %. Management should never evaluate marketing on raw leads alone.

3. Import & Customs (Q5 & Q6)

Best Margin & Landed Cost Report

Machinery has the best margin (NPR 3.75 Lakh profit, 8.72% on SP, 9.55% markup). Pickup is second at 8.65%.

Can we decide pricing solely on this? No. Excludes bank LC financing interest (8-10% p.a.), yard demurrage, PDI, warranty, and average 5% discounts which turn EV Vans unprofitable.

4. Collections & Aging (Q7 & Q8)

Critical Customer Priorities

1. ABC Construction (Top Priority): 0.60 Cr overdue (24% of AR), 115 days aging, 48% uncollected. Stop all deliveries.
2. Terai Cargo: 128 days overdue (0.24 Cr). High default risk.
3. Everest Transport & Mid-West Logistics: Both past 90 days with >32% uncollected.
Policy: Hard credit limits, 45-day stop-supply rule, and sales commissions linked to cash collected.

5. MANAGEMENT DECISION (Q9): OPTION A VS OPTION B

Between Option A and Option B, I prefer Option A (continuing imports to capture increasing customer demand and protect market presence), rather than shutting down imports completely.

HOWEVER, because the company has limited cash, I would NOT make a final decision blindly. Before deciding whether to slow down imports or proceed with Option A, the critical information I would want to look at is the CURRENT CASH FLOW STATEMENT and a 30-TO-60 DAY CASH INFLOW / OUTFLOW PROJECTION:

  • Check exact bank cash balance, upcoming Letter of Credit (LC) maturity dates, and customs duty liabilities at the border.
  • If the cash projection shows enough liquidity (or we quickly recover NPR 3-4 Cr from the 90+ day debtors), proceed with Option A on high-margin models (Machinery & Pickups).
  • If the cash projection shows an immediate cash deficit, only then would we be forced to temporarily slow down imports on low-margin units (like EV Vans) while recovering debt.

Management Strategic Dilemma: Option A vs Option B

Evaluating aggressive import growth vs collection freeze in a cash-limited environment

OPTION A: Import Additional Vehicles Candidate Preference

Rationale: Sales demand is expanding (12 units → 25 units). Halting imports hands market share to competitors and damages dealership reputation.

The Danger: Without cash visibility, blind imports cause bank LC defaults because LC and customs duties require hard cash while customers take 100+ days to pay.

OPTION B: Freeze Imports & Focus on AR Defensive

Rationale: Avoids taking on new LC liabilities and forces all operational energy into collecting the NPR 10.8 Cr trapped in receivables.

The Danger: Halting vehicle deliveries kills sales momentum, causes sales team demoralization, and fixed yard overhead still bleeds cash.

The Deciding Factor: Look at Cash Flow Projections

As a Data Analyst, my recommendation is: I prefer Option A, but management must review the 30-to-60 day Cash Flow Inflow vs Outflow Projection to determine whether we can execute Option A safely or if we are forced to slow down imports.

Condition 1 Cash Inflow >= LC Commitments

→ Execute Option A on high-margin Machinery & Pickups.

Condition 2 Recover NPR 3-4 Cr from >90d AR

→ Use recovered delinquent debt to self-fund next import batch.

Condition 3 Enforce 35% Cash Down Payment

→ Stop 100% open credit; collect cash before vehicle handover.
